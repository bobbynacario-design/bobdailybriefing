📡 Radar — what's worth a look today

Each card is one asset, ranked by a 0–100 score (higher = stronger setup right now on trend, volume and relative strength). Read a card top to bottom:

  • Score + status badge — the quick read:
    • confirmed — strong, signals lined up.
    • forming — building, not there yet.
    • invalidated — broke down / lagging. Don't chase.
  • The "why" sentence — why it's moving, in words.
  • 📰 catalyst chip — the recent news driving it (earnings, an upgrade, a macro event).
  • Plan line — Upside · Entry · Stop · Target · R:R. Upside is target distance from entry, useful for comparing reward size. The stop is the useful risk number: the level where the idea is wrong.

Taker Nuggets is the more opportunistic read — the names to press now. It pulls the best actionable signals from the current filter and names the stance. Confirmed structure earns priority, but a forming name already leading its benchmark can outrank a merely-decent confirmed one — that's edge showing up early.

  • Press strength — highest-conviction confirmed setups; scan these first if you want active opportunity.
  • Stalk breakout — forming setups with enough pressure to watch closely, but not fully confirmed yet.
  • Upside % — the estimated move from entry to target. Use it to compare reward size across nuggets, not as a probability that the target will hit.
  • Regime rationale — in risk-off tape, the box leans harder on visible relative strength and confirmed structure; forming names become stalks rather than chases.
  • Tripwire — the invalidation line. If that breaks, the aggressive read is wrong and the setup goes back to pass/reassess.

Wildcard Upside is the edge-before-it's-obvious box — early, deliberately non-consensus names. It never repeats a name already in Taker Nuggets, and it hunts the forming zone where relative strength and volume are turning up before the “confirmed” screen — the one everyone else runs — lights up:

  • Leadership-first — ranked by emerging relative strength, volume thrust and live catalysts, not raw target distance (that's only a capped tiebreak), so it tilts away from already-obvious top-of-screen names.
  • Adaptive asymmetry bar (bar ≥X%) — the minimum upside to qualify scales with the tape: low in a compressed risk-off field, higher when risk-on widens target distances. A name has to be unusually asymmetric for today.
  • Top upside — one slot is always reserved for the single biggest-upside qualifier, so a standout fat-tail is never crowded out by names that merely lead harder. The other two fill by leadership rank.

Higher variance and non-consensus by design — treat these as early watchlist ideas, not clean confirmations.

Quick filters (the pill rows under the regime line) narrow the list — they don't change any scores:

  • Asset type — All / Stocks / Crypto / Commodities. Each pill shows how many names sit in it. Commodities = metal/energy/broad-commodity ETFs (GLD, USO, GDX…); energy stocks like XOM stay under Stocks.
  • Status — the second row filters by confirmed / forming / invalidated, counted within the asset type you picked. It hides itself when there's only one status to show.

Use it as a shortlist and framing tool, never as buy signals. Check the regime line up top for the market backdrop, filter to the slice you care about, then scan the top confirmed/forming names and their catalysts. It tells you what's strong and why — never "buy this."

📓 Journal — has the radar actually been right?

The honesty check. It re-runs the score on the last ~60 days and measures what happened next. The one number that matters is excess — return vs the asset's own benchmark (QQQ / SPY / BTC):

  • Raw return — did it go up? (Easy in a rising market — everything went up.)
  • Excess return — did it beat what it should have, given its market? This is the real test. Raw can look great while excess is ~0.

Right now the journal says: raw returns look nicely ordered by score, but excess is roughly flat — only AI semis show real excess. Translation: the radar's apparent edge has mostly been riding the AI wave, not beating peers. That's the tool telling you the truth so you don't over-trust it.

The caveats line matters: ~60 days of mostly-correlated names is "indicative, not statistically significant." Treat the numbers as directional hints, not proof — they sharpen as more varied history builds up.

🎲 Markets — is a betting market mispriced?

Each card is one event market on Polymarket (e.g. "Will France win the 2026 World Cup?"). It compares the market's own price to an independent read, and tells you whether that's a real disagreement — or just noise. Read a card left to right:

  • mkt X% YES — the market's current price, read as the implied probability the answer is YES. This is the number to beat.
  • Panel Y% (haircut) — our estimate. 5 AI "personas" (a base-rate historian, an insider specialist, a contrarian, a newsdesk reporter, a calibrated generalist) each guess the probability without being told the price, so they're an independent read, not the price echoed back. We then haircut it — pull it most of the way back toward the market price — because a panel built from one model shares that model's blind spots and its confidence is partly cosmetic.
  • spread ± — how much the personas disagreed. A wide spread means low conviction, and the haircut pulls harder.
  • est. edge after costs — the gap that's actually left after the haircut and the bid/ask spread, with the side of the discrepancy (YES or NO). It's a research signal, not a trade ticket.
  • verdict badge — the research call:
    • RESEARCH FLAG — a real, liquid, agreed-upon discrepancy that clears costs. "Watch only — not advice."
    • NO EDGE / PANEL SPLIT / TOO THIN / SPREAD TOO WIDE / CLOSED — a check failed, and the badge says which: too close to the price, personas disagree, can't actually trade size, spread too wide, or already resolved.

The 📓 Journal banner up top is the honesty check. As markets resolve it scores our read against the market price using a Brier score (lower = better calibrated). Skill is the only number that matters: positive means we beat the price, negative means we didn't.

Expect mostly NO-GO, and expect skill near zero — these markets are close to efficient, so a handful of AI guesses rarely beats the crowd's price. That's the tool being honest: if even one check fails, you don't have a trade, you have a story. Nothing here is a bet or advice.

⚽ Sports — who is rising, and what does the market think?

The Sports tab starts with World Cup data. It combines fixtures/results with a simple 0–100 team momentum score. Higher means the team is currently showing stronger form, not that it is guaranteed to win.

  • Rising Teams — the strongest current movers by score. These are the first teams to scan if you want to know who is emerging.
  • Team Momentum — the full ranked table. Score is the model score, Form is recent W/D/L, and GD3 is goal-difference trend over recent matches.
  • Next Fixtures projection — a fixture-level lean based on both teams' momentum score, recent points, GD trend, goals for, goals against, and squad profile. Watch only is weak separation; Moderate edge / Strong edge are the only decisive projection buckets.
  • Squad Profile — roster age and position-balance from the World Cup teams feed. It helps stabilize early fixtures before form is rich, but it is not a player-rating, market-value, injury, or expected-XI model.
  • Projection Alignment — the feedback loop. It persists in the generated Sports document and replays completed matches point-in-time, using only matches before kickoff, then checks whether the projected lean matched the result. The headline uses Decisive accuracy for strong/moderate calls; weak Watch only calls are tracked separately so they do not dress up noise.
  • GF / GA chips — goals for and goals against per match. Rising with strong GF and low GA is better than rising only because of one messy result.
  • Tags — RISING means form and goal trend are improving; WATCH means promising but not fully confirmed; FADING means recent results or goal trend are weakening.

World Cup Market Lens finds where the Sports momentum signal disagrees with the Markets tab's World Cup outright prices. Both sides are re-based over the same set of teams (those with an outright market loaded), so the comparison is like-for-like — not a win probability:

  • Momentum % — the team's share of momentum within that matched set.
  • Market % — the team's share of market-implied probability within the same set. (Raw outright prices are on the Markets tab.)
  • Gap — momentum % minus market %. Positive = momentum ranks the team ahead of where the market prices it; negative = the market ranks it ahead of current form.
  • Read — FORM AHEAD, MARKET AHEAD, or IN LINE. A watchlist divergence to dig into, not an instruction to bet.

Best use: compare RISING teams with FORM AHEAD market reads, then check the fixtures and path. If a team is RISING but the market is already far ahead, the story may be real but already priced in.

The simple takeaway
  • Radar = today's framing: what's strong, why, and where it breaks. Use the status + stop + catalyst.
  • Journal = the reality check: look at excess, not raw. It currently says "we've mostly been long the AI trade" — so weight the radar accordingly.
  • Markets = is a betting market mispriced? Compare mkt % to our panel %; treat a RESEARCH FLAG as watch-only, and even then check the journal's skill. Mostly "no edge" is the honest, expected answer.
  • Sports = who is moving up or down. Use Momentum for form, and Market Lens for whether the market already agrees.
