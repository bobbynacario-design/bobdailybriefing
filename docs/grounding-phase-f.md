## What the roadmap assumed, and what the sources show

The roadmap plans an automated **watch** on the Fair Work Ombudsman's MA000020
pay guide, and a **manual** capture of the "CW1 ordinary hourly rate" in
`grounding/manual/fwo_ma000020_cw1_ordinary.json`. Three findings change the
details.

### 0. Update, same day: the pay guide itself is reachable, and non-commercial

- **The pay guide is generated by the FWO pay calculator,** not served from
  `www.fairwork.gov.au`. The pay guides page (read in a real browser) links
  `https://calculate.fairwork.gov.au/payguides/fairwork/ma000020/pdf` (and `/docx`).
- **That host answers Daybook's agent,** and its robots.txt says `Allow: /` for
  every agent. A HEAD request returned HTTP 200 (about 340 KB, but labelled
  `text/html`, to be confirmed on download).
- **So an automated watch on the pay guide itself is possible,** as the roadmap
  planned. Finding 1 below applies only to `www.fairwork.gov.au`.
- **Licence: FWO material is CC BY-NC 4.0** (non-commercial). Attribution is
  "© Fair Work Ombudsman www.fairwork.gov.au", plus the title, the URL and
  the licence link. The ABS was plain CC BY. ClaimBench uses its figures in paid
  assessments, so this needs Bob's decision (D-F5).
- **The Fair Work Commission publishes the same rates** through its Modern
  Awards Pay Database (MAPD) API, whose data is CC BY 4.0. Its catches:
  - it needs an account and a key, which Bob would have to create;
  - its terms forbid mirroring "any content available on the MAPD API on any
    other server", which sits uneasily with a public data branch.

### 1. fairwork.gov.au does not answer automated readers

`www.fairwork.gov.au`, which publishes the pay guides, accepts the TLS
connection (Akamai, `edgekey.net`) and then never answers. That held for:
- Daybook's user agent;
- a separate fetcher;
- one diagnostic probe with a generic browser agent, sent only to rule out
  agent-based blocking. The publisher never does that.

`www.fwc.gov.au` refuses Daybook outright (HTTP 403). Under G7 both are blocks
to respect. **The pay guide cannot be read automatically; it is captured by hand.**

### 2. The Commission's award text is readable, and already carries FY27

`https://awards.fairwork.gov.au/MA000020.html` returned HTTP 200 (1.97 MB) to
Daybook's agent.
- It says it "incorporates all amendments up to and including 1 July 2026
  (PR799301 and PR799458)".
- Clauses 19.1(a) and 22.1(a) are marked "ppc 01Jul26".
- It has no robots.txt of its own: `/robots.txt` redirects to the main site's
  home page, which does not answer.

It can serve as the **automated watch**. Once a day it can tell whether the award
has been varied for the new financial year.

### 3. The award puts traffic controllers at CW/ECW 2, not CW1

Schedule A lists "Traffic controller" among the indicative tasks of
**A.2.2 Construction worker level 2 (CW/ECW 2)**. It is the only place the award
names the role.

ClaimBench's approved FY26 record says **CW1, $29.74**. It comes from a
labour-hire firm's page (Harrison Barratt, secondary tier), which calls $29.74
"base (ordinary-time)".

The award defines the ordinary hourly rate as the clause 19.1(a) rate plus the
industry allowance, over 38 hours (clause 2, clause 19.3). For the general and
civil sector the allowance is $67.15 a week from 1 July 2026. From the award
text (FY26 figures approximated by removing the 4.75% rise):

| Classification | FY27 weekly hire | FY27 daily hire (×52/50.4) | FY26 weekly (approx.) | FY26 daily (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CW/ECW 1 (level d) | 29.82 | 30.77 | 28.47 | 29.37 |
| **CW/ECW 2** | **30.39** | 31.35 | 29.01 | 29.93 |
| CW/ECW 3 | 31.22 | 32.21 | 29.80 | 30.75 |

None of these reproduces $29.74 exactly, so what the FY26 figure measured is
unclear. These are **computed, not quoted**, so none of them can be a fact
(roadmap 5.2). The figure has to be read from the FWO pay guide, which states
each rate.

## Decisions for Bob

- **D-F1 Classification.** CW/ECW 2, as the award's Schedule A says (recommended).
  ClaimBench's FY26 record labels the role CW1 from a secondary source; that
  record is BI-Assessor's to review, and this finding goes to its session.
- **D-F2 Which rate.** The **full-time weekly-hire ordinary hourly rate**
  (minimum plus industry allowance). It is the true floor, and ClaimBench uses
  the figure only as a wage floor. Daily hire (+3.2% follow-the-job loading) and
  casual (+25%) would be higher. The alternative is daily hire, if Bob's traffic
  control invoices are for daily-hire labour.
- **D-F3 The watch source.** Read `awards.fairwork.gov.au/MA000020.html` once a
  day as Daybook (recommended), even though it publishes no robots rules of its
  own. Stop at the first refusal (a 401 or 403 turns the watch to blocked).
- **D-F5 Licence.** Accept the FWO pay guide's CC BY-NC 4.0 for this use:
  - the published record carries the attribution and licence;
  - the quote stays short;
  - the figure itself is the legal minimum wage set by the Commission.

  The alternative is the Commission's CC BY 4.0 MAPD API, with the account and
  mirroring caveats above.
- **D-F4 Capture logistics.**
  1. Bob downloads the FWO pay guide for MA000020 (the 1 July 2026 edition) in
     his own browser and gives me the file path.
  2. I write `grounding/manual/fwo_ma000020_cw2_ordinary.json` from it: quote,
     page, row and the file's SHA-256.
  3. Bob reviews the file before it is committed, which is what publishes it.

  Alternatively, I fetch the PDF in the app's built-in browser, with Bob's OK
  for that one download.

## Decisions taken (Bob, 2026-09-27)

- **Level: take it from the pay guide.** The FY27 guide labels no row "traffic
  controller". The 2025 edition could not be obtained:
  - the calculator serves only the current guide;
  - the Internet Archive holds only its "download will start shortly" page.

  Recomputed exactly from the award's rounding rules, no FY26 row gives $29.74.
  Bob then chose **Level 2 (CW/ECW 2), Weekly hire - full-time and part-time -
  Civil construction, $30.39**. That is the award's level for traffic
  controllers, and the true floor.
- **Licence:** accepted FWO's CC BY-NC 4.0, with its attribution carried in
  every record (D-F5).
- **Download:** Bob allowed the pay guide download. The real file is served by
  `/Download/AwardSummary?awardCode=ma000020&effectiveDate=&fileType=pdf&krn=`,
  which the page's own script fetches. It came back as `G00203138.pdf`, 1,692,067
  bytes, 99 pages, "Effective: 01/07/2026 Published: 02/07/2026", SHA-256
  `da4e9013…cda2e`. It is byte-identical on a second download.
- **The watch** is on the pay guide itself, as the roadmap planned: the
  calculator host allows robots. The award-text idea (D-F3) was not needed.

  guide as Daybook and compares its SHA-256 with the capture's:
  - the same guide says nothing, and the series reads as published;
  - a different guide turns it **stale**, naming the new file and date;
  - no capture yet reads as **awaiting**;
  - a response that isn't a PDF is **blocked**.

  The publisher now passes its manual folder to a watch.
- **Registry:** freshness 400 days, bounds 25–45 with a maximum change of 3
  (the same as BI-Assessor's), streams `tp_road` and `tp_utility`.
- **Tests:** 7 in `grounding/sources/fwo-pay-guide.test.js`, and 83 grounding
  tests in total. Seven deliberate breakages were each caught.

  release 000003, adding `fwo_ma000020_cw2_ordinary@2026-07-01#r1` = 30.39
